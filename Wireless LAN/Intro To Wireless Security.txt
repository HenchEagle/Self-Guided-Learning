**Introduction**

A wireless network, is a network that utilises radio signals to transmit data between devices.

It provides the freedom of movement, and the ability to extend applications to different areas of the building, city or world.

The first ever WLAN (Wireless Local Area Network) standard created by the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineering (IEEE) was the 802.11 Standard.


**Wireless Communication Explained**

Now this area took me around 30 minutes to wrap my head around. So I am going to try my best to explain it.

Wireless data transfer is performed through electro magnetic (EM) waves, specifically radio waves.

Radio waves operate at different bands, the current standards created by the IEEE operate at the 2.4GHz, 5GHz and most recently the 6GHz band.

By band we are simply refering to the frequency of the electro magnetic waves. For example, 2.4GHz reffering to a wave frequency of 2.4 Billion per second.

Now every single band has an allocated number of channels, these channels are blocks of 20 MHz. 
Each channel allows for unoverlapping communication, or simultaneous communication without interference

To understand this further, for example, the 2.4GHz band allows for communication of waves with a frequency between 2.4 and ~2.48GHZ. 
Each channel within that range is allowed a maximum of 40MHz, either 2*20 MHz or 1*2 0MHz. As the band contains a total spectrum size of 180MHz.

Although you may ask, why not 9*20 MHz.
Well, each channel is only space 5 MHz apart. What i mean is C1: 2400-2420 MHz, C2: 2405-2415 MHz, C5: 2425-2445 MHz etc.
Therefore channels between 1->4 are overlapping, while channel 5 is close enough to cause interference.
To conclude, to have a clean communication on the 2.4GHz band you must utilise only 3 of the non overlapping channels, 1, 6 and 11.

Below you can find a table of channel width, channel spacing, band size and spectrum size for each of the 3 standards.

**2.4 GHz**
Total Band Size: 180MHz (2400-2483.5 MHz)
Allowed Channel Widths: 20, 40 MHz
Channel Spacing: 5 MHz
Total Non-Overlapping Channels: 3*20 MHz (1, 6, 11) or 1*40MHz
Introducing IEEE Standard: 802.11 & 802.11b (Wi-Fi 1)

**5 GHz**
Total Band Size: 500 MHz (5.15-5.85 GHz) (150-200 MHz reserved for non WiFi uses.)
Allow Channel Widths: 20, 40, 80, 160 MHz
Channel Spacing: 20 MHz
Total Non-Overlapping Channels: 25*20 MHz, 12*40 MHz, 6*80MHz, 2*160MHz
Introducing IEEE Standard: 802.11a (Wi-Fi 2)

**6 GHz**
Total Band Size: 1200MHz (5.925-7.125 GHz)
Allow Channel Widths: 20, 40, 80, 160, 320 MHz
Channel Spacing: 20 MHz
Total Non-Overlapping Channels: 59*20 MHz, 29*40 MHz, 14*80MHz, 7*160MHz, 3*320 MHz
Introducing IEEE Standard: 802.11ax (Wi-Fi 6) with 802.11be (Wi-Fi 7 providing multi-link operations across all 3 bands.)

Several IEEE standards operate across several bands. Which band can be utilised by your hardware is dependant on the IEEE standard being utilised alongside the software
that is utilised for decoding the wireless data transfer. 


**Radio Interference**
Due to the nature of wireless communication, it is subject signal weakening and path loss as per their propegating properties. They are susceptible to influence from surrounding
objects such as walls, furniture and people which can absorb, reflect and scatter the waves reducing the signal of the data transfer.

Wireless communication is also affected by other radio signals, specifically those utilising the same / overlapping channels causing 2 waves of the same / similar frequency
to travel on top of each other creating noise turning once intelligible data into corrupted or lost packets.

**Wireless Antennas**
Antennas are a key component of wireless communication. There are 2 primary types of antennas:

- Directional      // Focuses the waves in a particular direction providing strong, long distance communication in 1 direction - weak communication past its 90-135 degree view.
- Omni-Directional // Creates a generally equal signal both for receiving and sending data around it in a donut shape.
