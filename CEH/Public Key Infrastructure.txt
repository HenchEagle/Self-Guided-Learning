**Introduction**
A Public Key Infrastructure (PKI) is a method of maintaining non-repudiation within an organisation.
It is a collection of software, hardware and services providing the ability to issue, manage and revoke digital signatures


**Key Components of a PKI**
- Certificate Authority
  A trusted entity that is responsible for issuing and signing digital certrificates.
  This entities signature is what provides the trust behind a certificate.
- Registration Authority
  A registartion authority acts as a middleman confirming the identity of clients requesting digital certificates.
  This prevents malicious or non reputable individuals / businesses from obtaining certificates from reputable CAs
- Digital Certificates
  A digital piece of documentation containing information about the owner of a digital certificate, typically containing information such as:
  Subject name, the corresponding public key, certificate usage settings
- Public & Private Keys
  Private keys are utilised to prove that an individual / service controls a digital certificate. It's like cross referencing a signature on a collectible with images of an original but with 100% guarantee.
  Public keys are utilised for encryption and verification of private key signatures.
- Certificate Repository & Database
  This is where digital certificates of all types are stored. Utilised by the Online Status Certificate Protocol (OSCP) - it also utilises Certificate Revokation Lists (CRLs)
  It provides a complete list of all certificates alongside their signing statuses "good", "revoked" and "unkown"
- Validation and Revocation
  As mentioned previously, this is performed by the OCSP which is a Layer 7 protocol running off HTTP. These communications do not require encryption
  because the data being transferred by this protocol is designed for integrity and authenticity.
- Certificate Management System 
  

The process to achieve complete organisational non-repudiation specifically for physical documents requires several steps. 
This complete form of non repudiation is referred to as a chain of trust, for document signatures its reffered to as a Qualified Electronic Signature (QES)

**The 3 components for achieving a complete QES:**

- Private key stored on physical hardware requiring a form of authentication to access by the singular named employee such as PIN or Biometric.
- Policies in place that require the individual to sign a contract stating the digital signature is the sole responsibility of safekeeping for the 
  named individual.
- Comprehensive, immutable logging that states files have complete integrity by not being edited by anyone other than the signed individual,
  typically achieved by storing a log of the individuals network activity with timestamps.

This is repeatable for areas such as Qualified Electronic Authentication (QEA) utilising 3 similar pillars, however not common in practice
for organisations due to the nature of having to re-enter these biometrics / pins every single time an individual attempts to access a protected
service such as VPN or website to prove sole control over the device. Therefore these services typically just utilise a standard digital certificate.
That doesn't provide complete legal non-repudiation, however sufficient logs that this user account accessed this service, at this time.
