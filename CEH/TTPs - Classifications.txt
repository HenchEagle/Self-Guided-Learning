For a cyber incident to be recognised as an attack it requires 3 seperate things to be present.
- A goal, or a motive for the attack.
- The method that was utilised for the attack to take place. (TTP, Tactics, Techniques and Procedures)
- A vulnerability that was exploited during the attack.



** Tactics, Techniques and Procedures (TTPs) **
This term refers to the patterns of activities and methodoligies utilised by threat actors, or groups of threat actors.

== Tactics ==
A strategy utilised by a threat actor to achieve the attacks motives

== Techniques ==
The technical methods that were utilised by an attacker to achieve results during the attack.

== Procedures ==
The systematic approach, through decomposition and pattern recognition utilised by the threat actors to launch an attack.

Analyising attack chains provides organisations with a fingerprint of a threat actor. This will show them what techniques
and strategies that they utilise, specifically for detecting repeat attackers.



** Classification of Attacks **
== Passive Attacks ==
These attackss do not tamper with, send or involve any type of data manipulation. Also referred to as passive reconnaisance. Examples
of this are sniffing and eavesdropping (the general act of listening to communication)

== Active Attacks ==
Tampering with data in transit, disruption of communications or attempts to bypass or break into secure systems. Examples include:
DoS, MitM, Session Hijacking and SQL Injection

== Close-in Attacks ==
This is a technique where attackers are in close physical proximity to a network, in order to gather, modify or disrupt access to information.
Examples include: social engineering, shoulder surfing or dumpster diving.

== Insider Attacks ==
Utilising privileged access to intentionally cause disruptions or pose a threat to organisations. Examples include: theft, keylogger planting, backdoors
or malware.

== Distribution Attacks == 
Distribution attacks are ones that occur when attackers tamper with hardware or software prior to installation. A prime example if this was when a 
popular system utility for fan configuration had a certain install shipped with malware. Attackers tamper with the hardware of software at its source
or in transit.



** Information Warfare
The term information warfare or InfoWar refers to utilising Information and Communication Technologies (ICT) to gain a competitive advantage
over an opponent.

There are always 2 sides, known as red team and blue team in a Defensive Warfare standpoint, focusing on prevention, deterrence, detection, and response.
While offensive warfare focuses on different types of attacks, system hacking, MitM, web service attacks, malware etc.
