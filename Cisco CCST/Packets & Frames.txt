Packets are handled at the network layer (L3), they contain information such as the IPv4 header and IPv6 header, along with the payload.
Frames are handled at the data-link layer (L2), packets are "encapsulated" with additional data such as MAC address

Encapsulation is the process of wrapping a packet into a frame, making it capable of being transmitted along a local network

** Crucal TCP IP Headers **
Source Port            | Randomly selected port on the local network (0-65535) to send the data on
Destination Port       | Not randomly selected port, opened by client specifically for communication - most likely TCP port 80
Source IP              | IP address of client
Destination IP         | IP address of server
TTL                    | Time-To-Live
Version                | IP version in use
Sequence Number        | Used to ensure integrity within data stream re-assembly
Acknowledgment Number  | Initial Sequence Number +1 to ensure that server/client have the correct sequence number
Checksum               | Integrity check
Data                   | The actual payload.
Flag                   | additional parameters, also sometimes known as options


** Three Way Handshake **
SYN     | The initial packet sent by the client to indicate it wants to begin a TCP connection and begin synchronisation.
SYN/ACK | The server sends a Synchronisation + Acknowledgment packet to indicate that it recognises the clients SYN and indicate readiness to start communication
ACK     | The client sends an acknowledgment packet to confirm the connection was successful.
DATA    | Once a connection has been established data such as files or bytes are sent within DATA packets
FIN     | A packet sent to gracefully close a connection indicating finished communication
RST     | A reset packet sent to abruptly terminate connection, often due to error or resource issues.


** Sequence Numbers **
SYN     | Here is my initial sequence number to SYNchronise with (0)
SYN/ACK | Here is my initial sequence number to SYNchronise with (5000). And I ACKnowledge your ISN (0)
ACK     | I ACKnowledge your ISN (5000) here is some data with my ISN + 1 (1)


** TCP Closing A Connection**
TCP will close a connection once the sending device determines the all data has been successfully received by the other device.
To initiate the closure of a connection the client will send a FIN packet. of course with TCP, the server must acknowledge this.
