Public Cloud - Publicly available infrastructure that can be offered for free or on a pay-as-you go basis such as cloud storage like google drive.

Private Cloud - Privately owned infrastructure dedicated exclusively to a single organisation. this infrastructure can be on and off premises

Hybrid Cloud - A combination of private and public cloud, such as private for sensitive information and public for normal data.

Community Cloud - Similar to public cloud, but designed for a specific group of organisations. for example health organisation must comply with certain legislations so their cloud software will be designed with this in mind.

When servers are underused and sit idle for a long amount of time, wasting energy and taking up more space than warranted by the amount of services it provides is known as server sprawl.

**WHY VIRTUALISATION**
- Less equipment required
- Lower energy costs
- Less physical space required

There are two types of hypervisors
--Type 1--
"Bare Metal" Approach
	- The hypervisor is installed directly onto the hardware, then instances of OSs are installed onto the hypervisor. 
	- This makes them more efficient than hosted architectures.

--Type 2--
"Hosted" Approach
	- Software that creates and runs VM instances such as VMWare Workstation
	- The computer on which the Type 2 hypervisor runs on is known as the host machines, type 2 hypervisors are also known as hosted hypervisors.

The advantage of type 2 over type 1 is that management consoles are not required.
